METHODS SECTION
================================================================================

**Bayesian Evidence Integration Framework**

We developed a context-aware Bayesian framework that integrates multiple lines
of evidence to generate confidence scores for peptide-HLA pairs. The framework
addresses two key challenges: (1) maintaining protein-specific evidence tracking
to prevent inappropriate validation transfer across homologs, and (2) integrating
experimental validation status with computational predictions in a probabilistically
sound manner.

**Model Structure**

Starting from a neutral prior probability (50%), we update confidence based on
available evidence using Bayes' theorem:

Posterior Odds = Prior Odds × Likelihood Ratio (LR) Product

where the LR product represents the multiplicative combination of independent
evidence types.

**Evidence Types and Likelihood Ratios**

*Primary Evidence: Experimental Validation*
- Experimental validation in immunological databases (e.g., IEDB): LR = 4-12
  (weighted by context relevance: target allele matching, tissue context)
- No experimental validation (predicted only): LR = 0.3
  (reflects high false-positive rate of computational predictions)

*Supporting Evidence (for predicted peptides):*
- Binding affinity predictions:
  * Strong binders: LR = 5
  * Intermediate binders: LR = 3
  * Weak binders: LR = 2
  
- HLA class matching:
  * Same class (Class I for HLA-A*02:01): LR = 2.0
  * Different class (Class II): LR = 0.5

- Target allele matching (when available):
  * Same allele: LR = 10
  * Different allele: LR = 2

*Context Weighting (for experimental evidence):*
- Normal tissue detection (safety concern): LR = 10
- Disease context match: LR = 6
- Tissue similarity: LR = 3

Evidence types combine multiplicatively following Bayes' rule. Final posterior
probabilities are classified into four confidence tiers:
- **High (>80%)**: Experimental validation with strong supporting evidence
- **Medium (50-80%)**: Strong computational predictions with supporting factors
- **Low (30-50%)**: Computational predictions with conflicting evidence
- **Very Low (<30%)**: Minimal supporting evidence

**Context-Aware Evidence Tracking**

Critically, the framework maintains protein-specific evidence profiles. Unlike
sequence-based aggregation approaches that pool evidence across all proteins
containing a peptide sequence, our method prevents inappropriate confidence
transfer. For example, experimental validation of KVDEJAVL in PABP1 does not
automatically confer high confidence to the identical sequence in PABP3 (see
Results).

This protein-specific tracking is implemented by maintaining separate evidence
profiles for each peptide-protein pair during preprocessing, with experimental
studies matched to specific protein contexts based on database annotations.

**Four-Tier Confidence Stratification Demonstrates Clear Evidence Integration**

The Bayesian framework analyzed 1,454 peptide-protein pairs derived from
[YOUR PROTEIN], stratifying them into four distinct confidence tiers with no
overlap between categories (Figure X).

**High Confidence (n=159, 10.9%): Mean = 99.74%**
These peptides have experimental validation in immunological databases, with
posterior probabilities ranging from 98.77% to 99.99%. All 159 high-confidence
peptides possess experimental data, with varying evidence quality reflected in
the 1.22 percentage point range:
- Multiple high-relevance studies (n=11): 99.98% ± 0.00%
- Single high-relevance studies (n=15): 99.97% ± 0.01%
- Multiple medium-relevance studies (n=82): 99.79% ± 0.12%
- Single medium-relevance studies (n=50): 99.55% ± 0.12%

Within this tier, evidence tiers document validation quality (study count,
context relevance) while all peptides merit high confidence for clinical
consideration. The tight clustering reflects our conservative calibration:
experimental validation represents strong evidence regardless of granular
tier distinctions.

**Medium Confidence (n=99, 6.8%): Mean = 64.29%**
These peptides lack experimental validation but possess strong computational
support: predicted intermediate binding affinity (LR=3.0) combined with HLA
Class I matching (LR=2.0), partially compensating for the predicted-only penalty
(LR=0.3). This tier represents priority candidates for experimental validation,
where computational predictions converge to support potential immunogenicity.

Calculation: Prior (50%) × Predicted (0.3) × Intermediate binding (3.0) ×
Class I match (2.0) = 1.8 odds → 64.29% posterior

**Low Confidence (n=1, 0.07%): Mean = 31.03%**
This single outlier peptide has predicted intermediate binding but HLA Class II
classification (LR=0.5 penalty), representing a context mismatch for our
HLA-A*02:01 (Class I) target. The class mismatch penalty reduces confidence
below both the prior and the Medium tier.

Calculation: Prior (50%) × Predicted (0.3) × Intermediate binding (3.0) ×
Class II penalty (0.5) = 0.45 odds → 31.03% posterior

**Very Low Confidence (n=1,195, 82.2%): Mean = 23.08%**
These peptides lack both experimental validation and strong computational support,
maintaining scores below the neutral prior due to the predicted-only penalty.
The model appropriately assigns low confidence pending additional evidence.

Calculation: Prior (50%) × Predicted (0.3) = 0.3 odds → 23.08% posterior

**Clear Separation Validates Model Function**

The four tiers demonstrate clear, non-overlapping stratification:
- High vs. Medium: 35.5 percentage point separation
- Medium vs. Low: 33.3 percentage point separation  
- Low vs. Very Low: 8.0 percentage point separation

No peptide falls between categories, confirming that the Bayesian framework
appropriately integrates evidence to produce discrete, interpretable confidence
levels. This stratification enables risk-based prioritization: high-confidence
peptides proceed directly to therapeutic consideration, medium-confidence peptides
warrant experimental validation, and very-low-confidence peptides require
substantial additional evidence.

**Protein-Specific Evidence Prevents Inappropriate Confidence Transfer**

Our context-aware approach maintains protein-specific evidence tracking,
preventing the inappropriate confidence inflation observed with sequence-based
aggregation. Among 94 peptide sequences appearing in multiple proteins, 4 showed
differential confidence classifications depending on protein context (see earlier
section on evidence specificity). For example, KVDEJAVL achieves High confidence
(99.8%) for PABP1/PABP4 where experimental validation exists, but Very Low
confidence (23.1%) for PABP3 where it lacks validation—demonstrating that our
framework correctly maintains context-specific evidence profiles rather than
inappropriately pooling validation across proteins.

DISCUSSION SECTION
================================================================================

**Four-Tier Confidence System Balances Precision and Interpretability**

Our Bayesian framework produces a four-tier confidence system (Very Low: 23%,
Low: 31%, Medium: 64%, High: 99%) with clear, non-overlapping stratification.
This structure reflects the primary distinction between experimentally validated
epitopes (High tier, n=159) and computational predictions (Medium/Low/Very Low
tiers, n=1,295), while maintaining granularity within the predicted category
based on supporting evidence strength.

**Rationale for Confidence Tier Calibration**

The 35-point separation between High (99.74%) and Medium (64.29%) tiers reflects
our conservative calibration philosophy: experimental database validation merits
near-certain confidence, while even the strongest computational predictions
(intermediate binding + class match) remain substantially below validated
epitopes. This conservative stance minimizes false positives in the High tier—
critical for therapeutic applications where validated epitopes represent lower-risk
targets.

Within the High tier, the narrow range (98.77-99.99%) reflects ceiling effects
when multiple strong evidence types combine multiplicatively. All peptides with
experimental validation achieve >98% confidence, appropriately reflecting that
database validation represents strong evidence regardless of granular quality
differences (single vs. multiple studies, high vs. medium context relevance).
These evidence tier distinctions remain valuable for:

1. **Provenance documentation**: Transparent reporting of which studies validate
   each peptide, supporting reproducibility and quality assessment
   
2. **Prioritization when resources are limited**: When selecting among multiple
   validated peptides (e.g., for vaccine manufacturing), evidence quality grades
   enable informed prioritization
   
3. **Framework extensibility**: As databases evolve to include quantitative
   immunogenicity data, our tier definitions can be refined without restructuring
   the framework

**Evidence Integration in the Predicted Category**

Within predicted peptides, our framework creates meaningful stratification
(23-64%) based on computational predictions and class matching. The Medium tier
(64.29%) identifies peptides where intermediate binding predictions and HLA class
concordance partially compensate for lack of experimental validation, creating
a prioritized list for experimental testing. The Low tier (31.03%) demonstrates
appropriate penalty application when evidence conflicts (intermediate binding but
wrong HLA class), while the Very Low tier (23.08%) appropriately assigns low
confidence to peptides lacking both validation and strong computational support.

This stratification within predicted peptides contrasts with binary approaches
that would treat all unvalidated candidates equivalently. Our framework enables
rational resource allocation: experimentally validate Medium-confidence candidates
first (strong computational support), while Very Low candidates require substantial
additional evidence to warrant experimental investment.

**Context-Aware Evidence Tracking vs. Sequence-Based Aggregation**

Our protein-specific evidence tracking prevents a critical failure mode of
sequence-based approaches: inappropriate confidence transfer across protein
contexts. As demonstrated by differential classification of sequence-identical
peptides (e.g., KVDEJAVL: 99.8% for PABP1 vs. 23.1% for PABP3), experimental
validation is protein-specific and non-transferable. This context-awareness
requires additional preprocessing complexity but prevents systematic overconfidence
for homologous sequences lacking direct validation.

**Limitations and Future Directions**

Our confidence calibration reflects current database limitations: experimental
data are largely binary (validated/not validated) rather than quantitative
(degree of immunogenicity). As databases expand to include T-cell response
magnitudes, patient outcome data, and negative results, our framework can
incorporate this information through refined likelihood ratios without structural
changes. Such refinement would likely create greater separation within the
current High tier (e.g., distinguishing strongly vs. weakly immunogenic validated
epitopes) while maintaining the primary validated/predicted distinction.

Additionally, our predicted-only penalty (LR=0.3) represents a conservative
calibration reflecting high false-positive rates in computational predictions.
Alternative calibrations (e.g., LR=1.0, neutral) would shift predicted peptides
toward higher baseline confidence (mean ~50% rather than 23-64%), potentially
more appropriate for discovery-focused applications prioritizing sensitivity
over specificity. Our framework supports such recalibration through simple
parameter adjustment.

**Clinical and Research Implications**

The four-tier system supports differentiated decision-making:
- **High confidence (99.74%)**: Validated epitopes suitable for immediate
  therapeutic consideration (vaccine candidates, adoptive T-cell therapy targets)
- **Medium confidence (64.29%)**: Priority experimental validation targets where
  computational predictions support but do not replace empirical testing
- **Low/Very Low confidence**: Lower-priority or experimental artifacts requiring
  substantial additional evidence

This graduated confidence framework enables appropriate resource allocation while
maintaining transparency about evidence sources and quality—critical for
translational applications where both sensitivity (identifying all true epitopes)
and specificity (avoiding false positives) carry clinical consequences.

RESPONSES TO POTENTIAL REVIEWER CONCERNS
================================================================================

**Concern: "Why is there so little variation within Medium and Very Low tiers?"**

Response: "The Medium tier (all 64.29%) represents a specific evidence
combination (predicted + intermediate binding + Class I match) that occurs
frequently in our dataset (n=99). These peptides receive identical evidence
chains, producing identical posteriors—a feature, not a bug. Similarly, Very
Low peptides lack additional evidence beyond predicted status, producing uniform
scores (23.08%). This reflects our data structure (categorical database evidence,
binary binding predictions) rather than model limitations. The High tier shows
more variation (98.77-99.99%) because experimental studies vary in context
relevance, though all achieve >98% appropriately reflecting validated status.

As computational predictions incorporate continuous binding scores or affinity
measurements (rather than categorical strong/intermediate/weak), we expect
greater within-tier variation. Our framework accommodates such refinement through
updated likelihood ratios without structural changes."

**Concern: "Isn't this essentially just binary classification (validated vs not)?"**

Response: "While the primary stratification is binary (validated: 99.74% vs
predicted: 23-64%), our framework adds critical value:

1. Context-aware validation tracking prevents inappropriate evidence transfer
   across proteins (see KVDEJAVL example: 99.8% for PABP1, 23.1% for PABP3)

2. Within predicted peptides, computational evidence integration creates
   meaningful stratification (Medium: 64.29% vs Very Low: 23.08%), enabling
   rational prioritization for experimental validation

3. Transparent provenance tracking documents which studies validate each peptide,
   supporting reproducibility and quality assessment

4. Extensible framework accommodates future quantitative data through parameter
   refinement

Binary classification (validated: yes/no) would lose this granularity while
providing minimal simplification benefit. Our approach maintains clarity about
primary distinction (validated vs predicted) while preserving evidence quality
information for informed decision-making."

**Concern: "The High tier range (98.77-99.99%) seems meaningless—all effectively 100%."**

Response: "We agree that for most clinical decisions, peptides scoring 98.77%
vs 99.99% would be treated equivalently (both merit high confidence). The
evidence tier distinctions within High confidence serve three purposes:

1. Provenance documentation: Transparently reporting whether validation comes
   from multiple studies in perfect target context (99.98%) vs single study
   with partial context match (98.77%)

2. Prioritization under resource constraints: When selecting among 159 validated
   peptides for limited manufacturing capacity, evidence quality grades inform
   risk-stratified selection

3. Framework extensibility: As databases incorporate quantitative immunogenicity
   data, current tier definitions enable refined calibration

We emphasize that the primary clinical distinction is High (99.74% ± 0.19%) vs
lower tiers, with evidence tiers within High serving documentation and
prioritization functions rather than changing treatment recommendations. This
parallels medical evidence grading (Level I/II/III), where all levels support
treatment but with different evidence robustness."
